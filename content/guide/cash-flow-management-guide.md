# Personal Cash Flow Management: Calculating Net Inflow, Burn Rate, and Runway

A common financial paradox occurs across every income bracket: **individuals earning $150,000 or $250,000 per year who still live paycheck to paycheck**.

The reason is simple: **High Income does not equal Positive Cash Flow**.

While *Net Worth* measures your long-term assets, **Cash Flow** measures the real-time velocity and volume of money moving through your life. If your cash outflows expand at the same speed as your earnings (lifestyle inflation), you remain financially fragile regardless of your salary.

This guide breaks down the core metrics of personal cash flow intelligence: how to calculate your true operational burn rate, measure net cash surplus, and extend your financial runway.

---

## 1. The Core Metrics of Personal Cash Flow

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------+
|                 PERSONAL CASH FLOW EQUATION                 |
+------------------------------+------------------------------+
| NET MONTHLY CASH FLOW        | = TOTAL NET INFLOWS (Income) |
|                              | - TOTAL OPERATIONAL EXPENSES |
+------------------------------+------------------------------+
```

### Metric 1: Net Cash Flow (Surplus or Deficit)
`Net Cash Flow = Net Inflows (Salary + Freelance + Dividends) - All Expenses (Fixed + Variable)`
- **Positive Surplus**: Capital available for index fund investing, real estate, and emergency reserves.
- **Negative Deficit**: Cash drain funded by credit card debt or asset liquidation.

### Metric 2: Monthly Burn Rate
Your **Burn Rate** is the total dollar amount required to maintain your living and working operations for 30 days.
- **Essential Burn Rate**: Non-negotiable survival costs (rent, utilities, basic food, debt minimums).
- **Total Lifestyle Burn Rate**: All expenditures including dining, entertainment, and travel.

### Metric 3: Emergency Runway (Months of Independence)
`Financial Runway (Months) = Total Liquid Savings / Monthly Essential Burn Rate`
- *Example:* If you have **$24,000** in a high-yield savings account and your monthly essential burn rate is **$4,000/month**, your runway is **6.0 months**.

## 4. Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between an income statement and cash flow?
An income statement records total revenue earned, while cash flow records actual cash received and disbursed. For freelancers with 60-day invoice payment terms, cash flow tracking prevents cash crunches while waiting for client payments (see [Freelancer Cash Flow Rules](/guide/freelancer-expense-tracking)).

### How much cash buffer should I keep in checking?
A recommended practice is keeping 1.0 to 1.5 months of typical living expenses in checking to prevent overdrafts, keeping the rest of your emergency reserve in a high-yield savings account earning competitive interest.
