## Key Anatomy of a Professional Expense Report

A complete expense report must provide both executive summary figures and itemized ledger proof:

1. **Header Information**: Company/Individual Name, Date Range, Reporting Currency, Unique Report ID.
2. **Category Totals Table**: Aggregate expenditures broken down by standard taxonomy (see our [Standard Expense Categories Guide](/guide/expense-categories)).
3. **Itemized Transaction Ledger**: Date, Payee/Merchant, Description/Purpose, Payment Method, Category, Amount.
4. **Reconciliation / Balance Summary**: Total claimed, taxes paid, and reimbursable subtotal.

## Best Practices for Frictionless Expense Filing

- **File Frequently**: Monthly reports are manageable; annual 12-month backlogs lead to lost receipts.
- **Provide Clear Business Context**: Rather than writing "Lunch", write "Client project kickoff with Acme Corp CTO". If you freelance, follow our [Self-Employed Tax Deduction Rules](/guide/freelancer-expense-tracking).
- **Standardize Currency**: If incurring multi-currency expenses during travel, ensure all amounts reflect the agreed base reporting currency (see [Multi-Currency Travel Expense Tracking](/guide/multi-currency-travel-expense-tracking)).
